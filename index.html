Numerical analysis for parametric and time-dependent PDEs

I am a postdoctoral researcher at the Institute for Analysis and Scientific Computing at TU Wien. My work combines rigorous approximation theory with computational methods for uncertainty quantification, model order reduction, boundary integral equations, and wave propagation.

FWF Principal Investigator Project

Advancing UQ and MOR for Computational Electromagnetics

This project develops mathematically certified methods for shape-parametric uncertainty quantification and model order reduction in computational electromagnetics, spanning time-dependent, high-frequency, and data-driven regimes.

Project: PAT1779526  ·  Funding: €446,086  ·  Host: TU Wien

National collaboration partner: Michael Feischl (TU Wien). International partners: Christoph Schwab (ETH Zürich) and Jürgen Dölz (University of Bonn).

View project on FWF Research Radar

Research interests

  • Shape holomorphy and high-dimensional uncertainty quantification for acoustic and electromagnetic scattering.
  • Model Order Reduction for time-dependent problems using the Laplace transform.
  • Boundary and volume integral equations for wave propagation and inverse problems.
  • High-frequency approximation and neural-network surrogates with frequency-robust complexity.
  • Adaptive methods and reduced dynamics for parabolic problems and micromagnetics.

Background

Before joining TU Wien in 2024, I was a postdoctoral researcher at EPFL in the Chair of Computational Mathematics and Simulation Science. I received my PhD in Applied and Computational Mathematics from ETH Zürich in 2021. My doctoral work, supervised by Christoph Schwab, studied shape uncertainty quantification in acoustic scattering.

My research connects operator analysis and high-dimensional approximation with computational methods that remain effective for complex geometries, long-time dynamics, and high-frequency wave propagation.
